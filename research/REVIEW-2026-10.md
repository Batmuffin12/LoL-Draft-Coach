## 1. Design: what problem we actually solve

### 1.1 What the evidence says about draft

| Evidence | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|

### 1.2 Verdict

**The spec frames the problem as "players lose games in draft". The evidence says that is only a small part of it.** Per game, for one player:

- playing an unfamiliar champion: **−6 to −13 pp** (the biggest single pre-game lever, in ~20 % of games);
- meta strength: ±2–4 pp; lane counter: ±2–5 pp; synergy: smaller;
- tilt / long sessions: a few pp, every session;
- everything else (macro, deaths, wave management) is decided in the game, and most players never improve without structure.

So the **real problem** for a Gold–Plat friend is: _"Don't throw away games before they start (unfamiliar picks, tilt, bad setup), and get measurably better one habit at a time."_ The draft-ranking engine is a means, not the product.

What this means for LoL Draft Coach:

1. **The product already points the right way** — comfort-first ranking, one-click loadout (table stakes, saves real time), growth focus, post-game card. The owner's own preference ("comfort first without a forced main") matches the evidence. Keep it.
2. **The meta engine's value is unproven.** On the only data tested so far it predicts no better than a coin (§5.3), and lane/counter terms almost never fire at today's volume (§3.1). Until a production backtest shows a gain, treat matchup/counter/synergy as tie-breakers and say so in the UI.
3. **Make the experience curve explicit**: "about 20 games to reach your usual level on a new champion" is the most useful, best-evidenced sentence the app can say, and it belongs in the pick card and the new-champion recommender ("try it in normals/Flex first").
4. **Be honest about stakes**: "draft gives a small edge; your play decides most games" builds trust and sends users to the growth features, where the bigger effects are.
5. **The cheapest high-value missing feature is a tilt / session guard** (own data only, fully compliant): after 2 losses in a row or N games in a session, show "your record after 2 losses: X %; a short break usually helps".

## 3. Data gathering

_Source: read-only trace of `apps/server/src/collector.ts`, `meta-job.ts`, `packages/riot-api`, `packages/meta`; read-only queries on the local DB; production `GET /health`; web research on Riot policy and outside data sources._

**Verdict: the pipeline is correct and compliant, but under-uses the key and is too thin for lane matchups.** Seeds come from League-V4 tier/division pages (not a snowball crawl, not users' own games), dedup is by match ID, remakes (< 300 s) and games with a missing role are dropped, roles use `teamPosition` (Riot's recommended field), and privacy holds (0 collector rows contain `puuid`/`riotId`/`summonerName`/`gameName`). Nothing is "weird" in the sense of wrong; the problems are throughput, staleness and patch blindness.

### 3.1 Measured volumes

| Source | Matches | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Production band 2 snapshot (2026-10-08) | **11,406** | ~2 days of hourly runs ≈ 215 band-2 + ~95 band-3 per run |
| Local DB, run #2 | 395 matches, 1,012 calls, 1,803 s | **2.56 calls per stored match** |
| Local data quality (559 ranked) | 16 remakes (68–269 s), 0 empty `teamPosition`, 0 duplicate roles | clean |
| Game age when collected (local) | p50 6.2 d, p75 16.4 d, p90 23.6 d | 30 % were already from the previous patch |

Projected lane-pair sample sizes (pick-rate model, rough):

| Band matches | Champion-roles ≥100 games (of 419) | Lane pairs ≥30 | Lane pairs ≥100 (of ~18k possible) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11,406 (today) | ~224 | ~264 | **~17** |
| 50,000 (cap) | ~308 | ~2,150 | **~460** |

Rule of thumb from Riot and stats sites: ±1 pp win rate needs ~10k games, ±2 pp ~2.5k ([Riot August](https://devtrackers.gg/leagueoflegends/p/8fc2d1be-riot-august-u-gg-data-is-garbage)); Lolalytics hides matchups under 100 games; 95 % CI ≈ ±1.96·√(0.25/n) = ±10 pp at 100 games. Typical matchup effects are 1–4 pp, so **today almost every lane/counter/synergy delta is the prior** — which is exactly what the backtest saw ("lane/counter almost never fire").

**Yes — there are too many percentages, and some contradict each other.** The numbers that actually change a decision are: the *order* of picks, the sign of the lane matchup, the personal ("You") penalty, and the ban threat. ### 4.2 Concrete problems (verified in the screenshot `picks-loaded.png`)

1. **One fact, four numbers.** "Ahri into Zed" shows **+1.1 %** in the Matchup column, **+2.2 %** in the reason line right below it, and 53.1 % / +2.2 % on the Matchups tab. The column merges `lane` + `counter` (`format.ts:91`); the reason cites lane only. A player reads this as a bug.
2. **The #1 pick is coloured as a loss.** Ahri shows **46 % in amber** (`winTone`, `format.ts:45`) next to a green **"Clear pick"** chip and the reason "No recent mid games on it" (You −6.0 %). The chip only measures the gap to #2 (`live.ts:334`) and ignores personal risk — exactly the factor §1 says matters most.
3. **Bans are empty on a cold start with a wrong message.** While history loads, `onDraft` returns early (`personal-coach.ts:633`) and the panel says "Ban suggestions need the live meta for your rank" (`ChampSelectScreen.tsx:104`) although the meta *is* loaded.
4. **The best draft-specific insight is hidden.** "How this game goes" (`power.laneAhead/late/early`, `live.ts:158-164`) exists only as a hover title on the YouStrip (`LockedInScreen.tsx:178`).
5. **"Win %" means three things**: predicted chance (Picks), historical win rate (Matchups/Pool/New), the enemy's win rate (Bans).
6. **Jargon**: "Vs avg", "win added", growth "win rate +43 points" (also implausible-looking), deaths per minute on Style vs deaths per game on Goal.
7. **Inconsistencies**: New tab says "strong in your rank" above Yasuo at 49.6 %; Month says "Your style, mid" while Goal and Style lead with jungle; the "Goals met" tile shows 9 but lists one (`MonthReport.tsx:176`).

## 5. Data analytics and tuning

_Source: code read of `packages/engine`, `packages/meta`, `config/engine.v1.json`, and a backtest run on a copy of the local DB._

### 5.1 How a pick is scored today (v2)

All terms are in rating points (Elo log-odds, `packages/engine/src/rating.ts:10-18`), smoothed as `(wins + k·expected)/(games + k)` (`rating.ts:26-28`).

| Term | Formula | Prior |
|---|---|---|
| meta | `w.meta·rating(smooth(champWR → 0.5))` − `offMetaPenalty` 20 (`live.ts:148`, `meta-index.ts:131-134`) | k=150, shrinks to 50 % (DESIGN §4 says: toward the champion's all-band WR) |
| lane (revealed) | `w.lane·[rating(smooth(matchup → E)) − rating(E)]`, `E = winOf(meta_a − meta_b)` (`live.ts:173-177`) | k=60 |
| lane (blind) | pick-share-weighted Δ over the top 15 opponents − 0.5·tail risk (`live.ts:178-197`) | k=60 |
| counter | Σ other revealed enemies `w.counter·0.4·boost·Δ`, boost 1.5 at last pick (`live.ts:199-210`) | k=60 |
| synergy | Σ allies `w.synergy·0.5·duoΔ` (`live.ts:212-221`) | k=60 |
| team | `w.team·(teamNeeds−0.5)·10`, hand-set (`live.ts:224-226`) | — |
| personal | `w.personal·max(−50, 160·(min(comfort,0.65)−0.5))`, unplayed −50 (`live.ts:74-78,229`); comfort WR shrinks to the **player's own average**, k=5 (`comfort.ts:43-51`) | — |

Confidence: "thin" if the top pick's meta n < 30, "clear" if the gap to #2 ≥ 1.5 pp, else "close" (`live.ts:331-334`).

### 5.2 Statistical problems

1. **Shrinkage is far too weak, especially for pairs.** The empirical-Bayes optimum is k ≈ p(1−p)/τ², τ = spread of *true* effects: ≈400–1,100 for champion WR, ≈300–600 for lane matchups, ≥2,500 for cross-lane counters and duos. Config uses 150 and one shared 60 for every pair type. The backtest prior sweep agrees: the best setting was the largest tried (1000/400). Fix: separate priors per pair type, τ² by method of moments, and `n_eff = (Σw)²/Σw²` instead of Σw for recency-weighted counts.
2. **Double counting.** (a) Comfort is measured against the player's own average, not the champion's band WR, so a good record on a strong champion counts in both comfort and meta. (b) The blind expected-lane mean is ≈0 by construction (meta already averages opponents); only the tail-risk part carries information, and that is a utility, not a probability. (c) `lastPickCounterBoost` 1.5× changes decision value, not predictive power, yet inflates `expectedWin`. (d) Counters are summed over 4 correlated pairs with an ad-hoc 0.4. → The shown "≈ 54 %" mixes a probability with decision penalties. Keep a calibrated `pWin` separate from a `utility` used only for ranking.
3. **Comfort is not on the meta scale.** Personal spans +26 to −55 points (+3.8 % to −7.8 %) vs meta ≈ ±15 and lane ±5–20 after shrinkage, so comfort dominates through hand-set constants (`comfortScale 160`). Mastery is a percentile *within the player's own pool* (`comfort.ts:97`), so half of every pool gets a negative personal term, even 300k-mastery champions. The direction is right (player-champion experience is the strongest pre-game signal in the literature); the magnitude must be fitted.
4. **Item "win added" is only partly de-biased.** Expected win is a minute × team-gold-diff table (`packages/meta/src/builds.ts:138-139`); ±1k gold is one bin, lane leads are invisible, completion time itself signals being ahead, late items exist only in long games, situational items follow enemy comps. Better: per champion-role logistic E(state) with lane gold diff and enemy traits, or compare against alternatives bought in the same slot/state.
5. **Confidence labels are not calibrated.** "Thin" ignores lane n, personal games and the runner-up; "clear" is a fixed 1.5 pp with no uncertainty. Fix: posterior variance per term → SE of total₁ − total₂ → "Clear" when P(#1 > #2) ≥ 0.8; verify by bootstrap stability of #1.

### 5.3 The backtest

`packages/meta/src/backtest.ts` + `apps/server/src/backtest-cli.ts`: log-loss, Brier, leaning accuracy, 2.5 % calibration bins, game-level bootstrap CI; time split (newest 25 % held out, snapshot built as of the cut); personal off. Problems:

- Priors and `teamRatingScale` are **chosen on the test set** (`backtest-cli.ts:76-87`): no validation fold, so "best" is optimistic.
- The item backtest uses `now: Date.now()` instead of the cut time (`backtest-cli.ts:96`).
- The unit is the *pick* (10 correlated predictions per game, each seeing only its own terms), not the draft; accuracy over "N leaning picks" overstates the sample.

**Run on the local DB copy (271 band-2 games, 68 held out):** v2 log-loss 0.6940 vs coin 0.6931 (CI [−0.0005, +0.0023]), 50.2 % right, all predictions within 47.5–52.5 %; every single term indistinguishable from chance; lane/counter almost never fire. Items: our top-1 47.8 % vs "most bought" 47.9 %. **The local DB is far too small to conclude anything; the production DB (11,406 band-2 matches) must be backtested.**

### 5.4 How to prove a tuning is (or isn't) needed

1. Rolling time split: train days 1–N, validate N+1, test N+2; fit priors/weights on validation only; report test once.
2. Draft-level model as the main object: `logit P(blue) = Σ blue terms − Σ red terms`, each matchup/duo once; one prediction per match.
3. Metrics: log-loss (primary), Brier, ECE (10 equal-mass bins) + reliability curve, AUC; **paired** game-level bootstrap for differences between models.
4. Baselines, in order: 50 %; side only (blue ≈ 51–52 %); champion WR only; DraftGap-like (meta + all pairwise matchups/duos); full v2. Ablate one term at a time from the full model.
5. Fit weights by L2 logistic regression on term values per band (keeps the additive, explainable form, as DESIGN §8 planned); refit k per pair type by validation grid.
6. **Sample size.** If true probabilities are 0.5+d with SD(d)=s, a calibrated model gains ≈2s² log-loss and needs N ≈ 7.84/s² games (80 % power): a 54 %-accurate signal (s≈0.05) → ≈3,100 games; 52 % (s≈0.025) → ≈12,500; telling 52 % from 50 % accuracy alone → ≈4,900. Production collects thousands a day, so **a few days of data is enough**; 68 games detects nothing.
7. **The personal part:** for each of a user's games, rebuild comfort from strictly earlier games only plus the band meta for that champion; pool users with a per-user intercept and fit one slope. Optionally store an anonymous *mastery bucket* on collected participant rows (no PUUID; check the no-brokering rule) to get an experience curve from thousands of games. `advice_log` followed-vs-not is confounded (players follow advice that agrees with them); use it for calibration only, or adjust for the terms.

### 5.5 What the numbers can honestly say

Draft-only solo-queue models land around 52–58 % accuracy; player-champion experience is the strongest pre-game signal (Do et al. 2021 report 75 % with experience features, an upper bound). Real differences between reasonable picks are ±1–3 pp (7–20 rating points). So: show "+1.5 % vs your usual pick" or words ("slight edge"), not "≈ 54 %" as if precise; hide deltas under ~1 pp.

## 6. Gaps in our thinking

Ranked by (evidence strength × value) / effort.

1. **We optimise the smallest lever.** Most engineering went into meta / matchup / counter / synergy terms worth ±2–5 pp and currently indistinguishable from chance at our volume, while the biggest pre-game effect (unfamiliar champion, −6 to −13 pp) is modelled with hand-set constants and a pool-relative mastery percentile that penalises half of every pool (§5.2.3). Fit the experience curve first; it is the core of the product.
2. **No proof the product helps.** There is no success metric. The advice log is confounded (players follow advice they already agree with). Define 2–3 product metrics the server can compute from data it already has: share of games on champions with ≥ 20 games; win rate on comfort picks vs off-pool picks per user; focus targets met per month; tilt nudges shown → whether the next game was played within 10 min. Review them monthly in the report.
3. **Tilt and session length are missing.** Strongest cheap evidence (48.8 % after 2 losses in Gold; breaks help; long sessions hurt — §1.1), own data only, fully compliant. Missing entirely.
4. **The growth focus doesn't target what holds Gold–Plat back.** Coaches agree on macro: deaths before ~14 min, converting won fights into objectives, CS@10/15. The user sync fetches no timelines (§2 spec drift), so "when you die" and "your gold@15 vs band" can't be computed yet. Adding own-game timelines (≈ +1 call per game) unlocks most of §5.6.
5. **Autofill is now a first-class situation.** Ranked 2026 added autofill parity (autofilled vs autofilled in 91 % of cases) and Aegis of Valor (double LP / protection when autofilled with ≥ C grade) ([Riot](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-ranked-2026), [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/23754/lol-autofill-matchmaking-hits-91-dodge-rate-drops-to-3)). An "autofilled mode" — safest low-learning-curve picks you've actually played in that role, plus "aim for a C grade to keep Aegis" — is a direct, well-timed feature. Also: you can no longer ban an ally's hovered champion — check the ban logic respects that.
6. **Flex and duo.** The group plays Flex (the only real recording is Ranked Flex), but the meta is solo-queue only; Flex ranks are being aligned with Solo/Duo in 2026. Duo is back at all ranks; friends share the server, so a consented "you with [friend]" record is natural (no League evidence on duo effects; low priority).
7. **Honesty about uncertainty.** "≈ 54 %" and four decimals imply a precision the model doesn't have (draft-only ceiling ≈ 57 %, real differences between reasonable picks ±1–3 pp). Calibrate (ECE on held-out games) before showing absolute chances; otherwise show deltas vs your usual pick in words.
8. **M8 compliance wording needs to be written down now.** Overwolf's Riot compliance page also prohibits power-spike notifications ("X hit level 6") and "notifications that dictate player action based on the current game state (e.g. go gank top lane)" ([Overwolf](https://dev.overwolf.com/ow-native/guides/game-compliance/riot-games)); third-party in-game ads are banned. In-game item advice must stay "options, not commands", on the player's request or at the shop — not pushed on game events. Add this to SPEC before M8.
9. **Scale assumptions.** The personal key is only for a "small private community"; any wider sharing needs a production key and Riot's approval, and every new feature (growth, simulator, report) must be described on the Developer Portal product page. Worth a checklist item at each release.
10. **Data volume is the binding constraint for everything statistical** (matchups, item win added, confidence labels). §3 shows we use ~29 % of the key. Throughput work (S effort) has a better return than any model change right now.
