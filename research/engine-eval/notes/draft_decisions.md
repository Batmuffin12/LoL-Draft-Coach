# Draft decisions: turning a win model into pick and ban recommendations (MOBA research, tools, evaluation)

Scope note: research date 2026-10-17. Source quality is marked inline: **[full text]** = read in the PDF or page itself; **[abstract]** = abstract only; **[snippet]** = search-engine summary only (the page itself could not be fetched); **[vendor]** = a company describing or evaluating its own product. Pro-play and solo-queue findings are kept apart throughout. "LDC" = LoL Draft Coach (the product this research is for).

## 1. How research systems turn a win-probability model into pick/ban choices when future picks are unknown (opponent model and evaluation)

### Takeaway
Every published drafting system scores a completed (or masked) lineup with a supervised win-rate model, then chooses picks by one of three opponent models: (a) a fully adversarial opponent via minimax or MCTS (OpenAI Five, DraftArtist/"Art of Drafting", JueWu-Draft, Tencent's 40-hero HoK agent); (b) an implicit "behavioural" expectation, where unknown picks are masked and the network averages over whatever followed in the training data (DraftRec, LoLDraftAI); or (c) no model at all: unknown enemies contribute nothing (DraftGap). Almost all of them were evaluated by simulated drafts scored by the **same** win model they optimise. Only DraftRec used a separate evaluator model plus a user study, and only the HoK full-game agent faced real opponents (with drafting and play mixed together).

### Cited Findings
**"The Art of Drafting" / DraftArtist (Chen et al., RecSys 2018; Dota 2) [full text]**
- Formulation: the draft is "a two-person zero-sum game with perfect information, discrete and sequential actions and deterministic rewards". The goal is the pick "that leads to the hero line-up with the largest predicted win rate on its team, assuming that both teams behave optimally in the remaining picks". Pick order is 1-2-2-2-2-1 (Ranked All Pick), and the reward is defined only on complete lineups: R1(s) = −R2(s) = w(s), the predicted Radiant win rate — [arXiv 1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130)
- Search: UCT (MCTS) with random-sampling rollouts. The authors note that UCT "converges to minimax's optimal action at a polynomial rate", so the implied opponent model is a rational adversary, approximated by search — [arXiv 1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130)
- Win model: 3,056,596 normal-skill Ranked All Pick matches (Feb 11 to Mar 2, 2016), 111 heroes. NN accuracy 0.65345 / AUC 0.71437; LR 0.63576 / 0.68767; GBDT 0.64172 / 0.70142; majority class 0.53779 (Radiant won 53.75%). The authors warn that the small LR–NN gap (1.8 points accuracy, 0.03 AUC) "may give a wrong impression that match outcomes are accountable by the sum of individual heroes' effects", and attribute it to players already building "closely competitive hero line-ups" — [arXiv 1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130)
- Evaluation: 1,000 simulated drafts per pair of strategies. The first pick is sampled by pick frequency, each strategy picks first in half the drafts, random seeds are shared, and the metric is the mean **predicted** win rate from the same NN used as the MCTS reward. UCT(1600 iterations) beat highest-win-rate (HWR) 61.1% in All Pick and 62.7% in Captain's Mode, but beat UCT(800) only 51.3% (diminishing returns). HWR beat UCT(200) 51.6%. Association rules beat random 68.2% (the original authors reported 76.4%). UCT1600 took 1.12 s per pick; HWR took 0.1 ms — [arXiv 1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130)
- Bans (Captain's Mode, 22 steps): bans become actions in the same game tree. The association-rules baseline bans "as if the opponent is to pick"; HWR bans the highest-win-rate available hero — [arXiv 1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130)
- Stated limitation: no player-specific information. "a hero recommended by our algorithm ... may be poorly played by a player who is not familiar with it" — [arXiv 1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130). Code: [czxttkl/DraftArtist](https://github.com/czxttkl/DraftArtist) (last pushed 2018)

**JueWu-Draft (Chen, Zhu, Ye, Zhang, Fu, Yang; Tencent; arXiv v4 Aug 2021; IEEE ToG) [full text]**
- Formulation: drafting as "a two-player zero-sum game with perfect information", extended to best-of-N series where a team cannot reuse its heroes. HoK pick order 1-2-2-1-1-2-2-1-1-2. "in this paper we do not consider banning heroes"; bans are listed as limitation #1 — [arXiv 2012.10171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10171)
- Method: AlphaZero-style PUCT MCTS with a policy+value network (3-layer MLP) instead of random rollouts. A separate 3-layer win-rate predictor scores terminal lineups. The reward is the sum over rounds of the transformed round win rates, (φ−0.5)×2, with a "long-term value" back-up for later rounds — [arXiv 2012.10171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10171)
- Data and predictor quality: an AI self-play dataset (30 M matches, 95 heroes) and a human dataset (30 M matches by the top 5% of players, Aug 1–10 2020). On human data the NN reached accuracy 0.617 / AUC 0.694 (LR 0.568 / 0.642; GBDT 0.561 / 0.648); on AI data 0.847 / 0.908. Hero mean win rates span 41.4–55.4% in human data versus 26.4–81.9% in AI data — [arXiv 2012.10171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10171)
- Evaluation: 1,000 simulated drafts per pair, scored by "the averaged winning rate predicted by φ(s)", the same predictor used for training. About 1 s per pick (1.6×10⁴ iterations for BO1). On human data, BO1: JueWu vs DraftArtist 0.525, vs HWR 0.600, vs random 0.756. BO3: vs DraftArtist 0.558, vs JueWu without long-term value 0.520. BO5: vs DraftArtist 0.547. Ablation (BO3, human data, vs HWR): plain MCTS 0.571 → +value network 0.581 → +policy and value 0.618 → +long-term value 0.642 — [arXiv 2012.10171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10171)
- Stated limitations: no bans; the predictor is trained on RL or human matches while the system plays humans, and it "does not explicitly consider this gap"; one predictor cannot model different teams' styles; maximising mean round win rate is "a imperfect approximation". Deployed to pick heroes for the AI side in a PvE mode — [arXiv 2012.10171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10171)

**Tencent full-game HoK agent (Ye et al., NeurIPS 2020) [full text]**
- 40-hero pool. Minimax was judged intractable, so drafting uses UCT MCTS with a value network ("similar to AlphaGo Zero"). The value network was trained on 100 M samples from MCTS-vs-MCTS simulated drafts, labelled by a win-rate predictor trained on 30 M self-play matches. Over 1,000 matches per pair, MCTS drafting beat random 0.86 and HWR 0.68 in mean win rate. The text says "matches played" but does not make clear whether these were real AI-vs-AI games or predictor estimates — [arXiv 2011.12692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12692)
- The whole AI (drafting plus play) won 95.2% of 42 matches against professionals and 97.7% of 642,047 matches against High King players. This does not separate drafting quality from play quality — [arXiv 2011.12692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12692)

**OpenAI Five (Dota 2, 2019) [full text, Appendix D.2]**
- The agent trained on random heroes, so no drafting agent was trained. Instead, its own win-probability head at the first frames of a game was precomputed for all 4,900,896 lineups of the 17-hero pool. "a dynamic programming algorithm" drafts "the one that maximizes worst-case scenario of opponent hero selection (minimax algorithm)". Example: the humans' first pick moved the agent's estimate from 52.8% to 65.1%. Before any pick, Radiant was estimated at 54% if picking first and 53% if picking second. No systematic evaluation of draft quality was reported — [arXiv 1912.06680](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06680)

**DraftRec (Lee et al., WWW 2022; LoL and Dota 2) [full text]**
- A hierarchical Transformer: a player network encodes each player's match history (champion, role, in-game stats), and a match network combines all ten players. Two heads, trained jointly: a pick distribution ("policy") and a win probability ("value"). Unknown slots are "[unk]"/"[mask]" tokens. To score candidate c, c is put in the current slot and the next slot is masked, so future picks are averaged implicitly, the way the training data averaged them, with no search — [arXiv 2204.12750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12750)
- Recommendation rule (DraftRec+): argmax of predicted win probability among champions whose predicted selection probability exceeds τ = 0.02 (≈ "champions that were selected within the most recent 50 previous matches"). The rationale: value predictions on unseen states "might have arbitrarily inaccurate prediction values", so the decision space is restricted to the training distribution, as in safe offline RL — [arXiv 2204.12750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12750)
- Data: LoL, 279,893 matches by the top 0.1% (Jun 1 to Sep 9 2021), 156 champions, 62,466 players, average 66.38 history matches per player. Dota 2: 50,000 Kaggle matches — [arXiv 2204.12750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12750)
- Evaluation: (i) pick prediction (HR@k/NDCG, see section 4); (ii) outcome prediction; (iii) offline strategy evaluation with a **separate** evaluator model (OptMatch), because "If we use an identical model for both of our recommendation and match outcome evaluation, the correlation of inaccurate predictions can be problematic"; online A/B testing was judged "prohibitively expensive and may hurt players' in-game experiences"; (iv) a user study with N = 84 — [arXiv 2204.12750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12750). Code: [dojeon-ai/DraftRec](https://github.com/dojeon-ai/DraftRec)

**Commercial LoL models (how they handle unknown picks)**
- LoLDraftAI: missing champions get an UNKNOWN token; "Training randomly masks picks from real games". Each candidate champion is inserted into a separate draft and the candidates are ranked by resulting win rate. "Because the Riot data lacks pick order, the model cannot learn 'blind pickability'" **[vendor]** — [LoLDraftAI explained](https://loldraftai.com/blog/loldraftai-explained). Code archive (May 2025 state): [Looyyd/loldraftai-monorepo-public](https://github.com/Looyyd/loldraftai-monorepo-public)
- DraftGap (open source): `getSuggestions` loops over every champion × every remaining role, inserts it, runs `analyzeDraft` on the **known** picks only, and sorts by draft win rate. Unknown enemies add no term, which amounts to assuming an average opponent — [draftgap suggestions.ts](https://github.com/vigovlugt/draftgap/blob/main/packages/core/src/draft/suggestions.ts), [analysis.ts](https://github.com/vigovlugt/draftgap/blob/main/packages/core/src/draft/analysis.ts)
- iTero (2022): the model predicts the winner from both drafts; "Then, it's just a case of brute force searching all options (around 160 Champions...)" **[vendor; snippet for the brute-force quote]** — [iTero draft model](https://itero.gg/articles/the-draft-model)

**Game-theoretic and RL/AlphaZero work**
- I found no paper that computes a Nash equilibrium for MOBA drafting. The zero-sum perfect-information framing in DraftArtist and JueWu implies minimax solutions. BPCoach (CSCW 2024) plans pro best-of-N ban/pick with visual analytics; per a search summary it uses MCTS, Markov chains and a Random Forest, and was evaluated with case studies, expert feedback and a user study **[abstract + snippet]** — [arXiv 2311.05912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05912)
- A search found no 2023–2025 AlphaZero-style self-play drafting paper beyond the Tencent work; a 2024 self-play survey covers only OpenAI Five and HoK in its MOBA section **[snippet]** — [arXiv 2408.01072](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01072)

### Inferences
- The fully adversarial (minimax/MCTS) opponent model fits pro play and AI self-play, where both sides optimise the same win model. It fits solo queue poorly: picks are driven by personal pools (section 4: a player's own history predicts ~50% of their top-1 picks), and the coach controls one of the ten picks. Searching ten plies to "beat" an optimal opponent overstates counter-pick threat and adds compute without a matching gain.
- In-model simulated evaluation (DraftArtist, JueWu, HoK) mostly measures how well a search exploits its own reward model. JueWu's gains shrink sharply moving from AI data (wide hero spreads) to human data (e.g., vs HWR 0.679 → 0.600 in BO1). Expect even smaller real effects in LoL solo queue, where draft-only predictors reach only ~53–56% accuracy (section 2).
- LDC's engine is structurally a DraftGap-style additive Elo model with two additions DraftGap lacks: a blind-pick expectation over likely opponents and a dominant personal term. The research suggests the principled form of the blind term is a **one-ply expectimax**: expected win probability over the distribution of the enemy laner's pick, conditional on that enemy seeing LDC's pick when they pick later (section 2). Full minimax or MCTS is not required.
- **Guidance:** keep the additive model (interaction gains are small, see section 2). Compute each candidate's score as E over unknown, later-picking enemies of the rating sum, using pick priors that are availability- and role-adjusted (section 4). This replaces the ad-hoc multipliers (the ×1.5 at last pick and the 0.5 × tail risk) with quantities that become exact as information arrives: at last pick the expectation collapses to the known matchup, so no multiplier is needed.

### Gaps
- No published Nash or equilibrium analysis of LoL's **simultaneous** ban phase or of solo-queue drafting was found.
- The IEEE ToG version of JueWu-Draft was not checked against the arXiv v4 (the numbers above are from arXiv v4).
- The HoK NeurIPS paper does not make clear whether the 0.86/0.68 drafting results are played games or predictor estimates.

## 2. Blind pick vs counter pick: valuing safety and counter-ability; measured pick-order and side effects

### Takeaway
Research systems value blind safety implicitly: either through search (worst case after the opponent's reply) or through a model trained on masked drafts. LoLDraftAI admits that public Riot data **has no pick order**, so blind-pickability cannot be learned or measured from match-v5 data. The only 2026 solo-queue estimate of counter-pick value is model-based: a last picker could gain about +3.8% (realistic baseline) to +7.3% (naive) predicted win rate by taking LoLDraftAI's top suggestion. Side effects in solo queue are small and vary by region and rank (EUW red ≈ 51.3%; Master+ red 53% in mid-2024). No Gold–Platinum split by side, and no measured solo-queue win rate by pick position, was found. Pro play shows a small blue edge historically (51.9%, 2020–22) that varies by league and year.

### Inferences
- **Safety is not variance.** A ranked game has a binary outcome, so a risk-neutral player maximises expected win probability. The spread of matchup outcomes does not lower expected LP. "Blind-pick risk" therefore matters only through (a) **adversarial selection**: an enemy laner who picks later can see the pick and counter it, so the relevant opponent distribution is tilted toward counters relative to raw pick share; and (b) **estimation uncertainty** in thin matchup cells. LDC's "0.5 × tail risk" stands in for (a). A principled replacement is a quantal-response tilt: P(enemy c | my pick m) ∝ prior(c | role, available) × exp(β · Δ(c vs m)). β = 0 gives population pick share; β → ∞ gives minimax (OpenAI Five / DraftArtist). β cannot be fit from match-v5 data (no pick order), but LDC's desktop app can see pick order and hovers through the LCU for its own users' games, which allows a rough check over time. Handle (b) with shrinkage (DraftGap shrinks matchups toward the additive expectation with 250–3,000 prior games; see section 4).
- **Counter-pick value at last pick is modest.** The realistic baseline in LoLDraftAI's study is flat at ~+3–4 points across all ten picks, while the naive baseline rises to +7.3%. Much of the apparent last-pick bonus comes from the information revealed, not from a multiplier. In an expectation framework, the counter term grows automatically as enemies lock in. LDC's fixed ×1.5 last-pick multiplier on counters vs non-lane enemies is not supported by any source found; calibrate it on held-out games or drop it.
- The draft explains only a few points of win probability in solo queue (best models ~55–56% accuracy; calibrated buckets above 60% are rare), and pairwise interactions add ~1 point over additive models. LDC's top-3 picks will typically differ by a fraction of a point in draft value, so the personal/comfort term will usually decide the ordering. That is consistent with the owner's "comfort first" preference.
- **Side:** treat side as at most a ±1–3 point, region- and rank-dependent constant that does not change which champion is best. It is irrelevant to relative pick ranking unless side-specific champion data were collected. Don't show side-based advice to Gold–Plat EUW users without LDC's own band data (the ~50k-game band can measure side win rate directly; matchmaking can confound it).

### Gaps
- No published, outcome-based solo-queue win rate by pick position (first vs last pick) for 2023–2026 was found. It cannot be computed from match-v5, which lacks pick order. LCU-based apps could measure it but none has published.
- No Gold or Platinum side win rate by rank was found (League of Graphs and LoLProfile per-rank pages returned 403).
- Riot's original 2020 side-balance post and any 2023–2026 official side data were not located.

## 3. Ban recommendation: methods, how tools present bans, and evaluation of ban advice

### Takeaway
The published and commercial ban methods reduce to four families: (1) **global threat**: the highest win-rate or highest "pick-ban influence" champion; (2) **expected threat**: harm to my team × probability the enemy actually picks it (LoLTheory; Lolalytics' PBI formula is the global version and divides pick rate by (100 − ban rate)); (3) **protect my pick**: ban the counters to my hovered or planned champion (ProComps' solo-queue "prepick counters", LoLTheory's hover handling); (4) **search**: bans as moves in the MCTS tree (DraftArtist Captain's Mode). JueWu omits bans entirely. I found **no evaluation of ban advice**, academic or commercial.

### Cited Findings
- DraftArtist (Captain's Mode): bans are tree actions. The baselines are "AR ... identifying candidate heroes to ban as if the opponent is to pick" and HWR, which "always chooses among the available heroes with the highest win rate for both the pick and ban actions". UCT1600 beat HWR 62.7% (vs 61.1% without bans), and "the advanced planning brought by the MCTS algorithm could further widen its gap over baseline methods in more sophisticated settings" (in-model predicted win rate) — [arXiv 1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130)
- JueWu-Draft: "we have not considered about banning heroes yet in this algorithm" — [arXiv 2012.10171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10171)
- Lolalytics tier list shows Win, Pick, Ban and **PBI** (Pick Ban Influence) = (win − AvgWinofTier) × 100 × pick / (100 − ban). Its tooltip says high-PBI champions are "likely contested picks and strong global ban targets". Tiers use win rate, PBI and best-player performance (Emerald+, Solo/Duo, patch 16.20 at fetch time) — [Lolalytics tier list](https://lolalytics.com/lol/tierlist/)
- LoLTheory: bans weigh "how well the enemy champion performs against your team" and "how likely the enemy is to actually pick it", account for teammates' current hovers, and estimate "the chance enemies won't play their expected lane" **[vendor; no formula]** — [What is LoLTheory](https://blog.loltheory.gg/what-is-loltheory/)
- ProComps (Sep 2023): detects solo/duo vs tournament mode automatically. In Solo/Duo only two factors apply: **Prepick Counters** (threats to your chosen champion) and **Tier**. Tournament mode adds enemy synergy, "Complements Team On" (gaps in the enemy comp) and enemy-ban counters in its second ban phase. It shows the "top 5 champion suggestions", "Presented in order of relevance", per lane or for all lanes, with a breakdown of reasons **[vendor]** — [ProComps ban suggestions](https://hub.procomps.gg/2023/09/ban-suggestions-feature/)
- iTero's app "calculates which of your champions would perform best. This also includes recommending champions to ban" (method not published) **[vendor; snippet]** — [itero.gg](https://itero.gg)
- Mobalytics publishes static "most banned" lists (e.g., patch 14.6, Emerald+) with qualitative reasons: counters, meta strength, frustrating kits **[snippet]** — [Mobalytics most banned](https://mobalytics.gg/lol/guides/10-most-banned-champs). Porofessor shows ban suggestions during the draft alongside counterpick tips **[snippet; page returned 403]** — [Porofessor](https://go.overwolf.com/porofessor)
- Community heuristics: "high win rate plus high pick rate" makes a champion ban-worthy when nothing is known about the enemy — [Mobafire blog](https://www.mobafire.com/profile/vexroth-588862/blog/bans-by-rank) **[snippet]**. A regression of ban rate on win rate and pick rate flagged Samira, Darius and Morgana as most "over-banned" relative to their strength — [devtrackers](https://devtrackers.gg/leagueoflegends/p/50ad8e07-data-the-most-hated-champions-are-samira-darius-and-morgana-the-most-liked-champions-are-jhin-ezreal-and-ashe-here-is-the-math-and-the-full-list) **[snippet]**
- Pro pattern: red is "forced" to ban strong first-pick champions because blue picks first (Lucian, Pantheon examples above) — [iTero side selection](https://www.itero.gg/articles/side-selection)

### Inferences
- For one solo-queue player banning before any pick, the consistent formulation across sources is **expected threat**: ban value(c) = Σ over enemy roles r of P(enemy picks c in r | available) × harm(c, r), where harm is the drop in LDC's predicted win probability. P uses the availability-adjusted pick share (pick/(1 − ban), as in PBI), and harm weights the lane opponent of the player's planned picks most, then other enemies. Lolalytics' PBI is the special case "harm = global win-rate edge, all roles".
- "Protect my pick" is the conditional version: given the player's likely picks (hover, or LDC's top-3 weighted by how likely the player is to take each), harm(c) is the expected loss to those picks. LDC's current rule ("counters to the picks we recommend and strong champions in the player's role; hovering adds bans that protect it") combines families (2) and (3). It is missing an explicit P(enemy picks it) weight. A strong but rarely played counter should rank below a slightly weaker but common one.
- Expected reduction of the opponent's draft value (the search view) adds little in solo queue: the five enemies' bans and picks are not coordinated against you. A ban's marginal value is also lower when a teammate is likely to ban the same champion (duplicate bans are wasted). If teammates' ban intents are visible in the ban phase (not verified here), LDC can drop those champions from its list.
- **Presentation guidance:** show 3 bans, each with a one-line reason tied to a number the engine produced ("counters your Ahri hover: −2.1 pts in lane, picked in 8% of mid games"). Group bans by intent (protect your pick vs strongest threat), as ProComps does with "Prepick Counters" vs "Tier". Respect the rule that an ally's hovered champion can't be banned.

### Gaps
- No evaluation of ban advice (simulated, observational or user study) was found in the literature or from vendors.
- The ban logic of U.GG, OP.GG, Blitz, Mobalytics' app, Porofessor, League of Graphs and winrate.gg could not be confirmed. Their pages returned 403 or no methodology turned up, and winrate.gg returned no results at all.
- Whether solo-queue ban hovers of teammates are visible during the ban phase was not verified.

## 4. Opponent pick modelling: priors, ban adjustment, role uncertainty and pick-prediction accuracy

### Takeaway
Predicting an anonymous player's next pick is hard. Without that player's history, the best published LoL model (DraftRec, top 0.1%) gets about **15% top-1, 40% top-5 and 57% top-10**. With the player's own history it rises to ~50% / 80% / 88%. In pro Dota 2 and Heroes of the Storm, the pick is in a model's top 3 only 12–30% of the time. Since Riot hides player identity and stats in solo-queue champion select, an enemy should be modelled as a **distribution**: role-conditional pick share, adjusted for bans/availability and for role uncertainty. Point predictions are not useful.

### Cited Findings
- DraftRec, LoL top 0.1%, next-pick prediction (HR@1 / HR@5 / HR@10). Full model with player histories 0.5042 / 0.8025 / 0.8836. **Without player history** (champions, roles, team and turn only) 0.1456 / 0.3950 / 0.5675. Player's own most-frequent champions (POP) 0.3212 / 0.5646 / 0.6553; most recent (S-POP) 0.4135 / 0.7404 / 0.8469. Dota 2 (sparse histories) is much lower: DraftRec HR@10 0.2547 — [arXiv 2204.12750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12750). (Rows were re-aligned from the PDF table and checked against the paper's stated +12.1% HR@1 and +8% HR@5 over SASRec.)
- Summerville, Cook & Steenhuisen (AIIDE 2016), pro Dota 2 (1,518 matches, Oct–Dec 2015, 111 heroes, picks and bans): "the LSTMs had the highest accuracy with a success rate of 11.94% on the held out test data". The figure caption describes the metric as "Average accuracy of the Top 3 picks". The LSTM was "up to 2.4× as accurate" as a Bayes net on the final pick. Human commentators: Lenient 31.48%, Strict 13.11%, All Possible 6.66% (figure values re-aligned from a garbled PDF extraction). Sequence models predict "what is likely to be picked, not what is necessarily best" (as quoted by DraftArtist and JueWu) — [AIIDE 2016](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12899)
- Gourdeau & Archambault (IEEE ToG 13(4) 2021, online 2020): on professional games the actual pick was in the drafter's top 3 for 30.4% (Heroes of the Storm) and 17.6% (Dota 2), "exceeding all previously reported results" **[abstract]** — [IEEE Xplore 8986550](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8986550)
- Ban/availability adjustment in practice: Lolalytics' PBI multiplies by pick / (100 − ban), i.e. pick rate among games where the champion was available — [Lolalytics tier list](https://lolalytics.com/lol/tierlist/)
- Role uncertainty: DraftGap's role predictor enumerates every assignment of unassigned champions to free roles. Each assignment's probability is the product of each champion's role share (games in role / total games), normalised to per-champion role probabilities — [draftgap role-predictor.ts](https://github.com/vigovlugt/draftgap/blob/main/packages/core/src/role/role-predictor.ts). LoLTheory models "the chance enemies won't play their expected lane" **[vendor]** — [What is LoLTheory](https://blog.loltheory.gg/what-is-loltheory/)
- Thin-cell shrinkage (DraftGap): a champion's patch win rate is pooled with priorGames × (30-day win rate). Matchup and duo cells are pooled toward the expected value from the two champions' ratings. Both directions of a matchup (A vs B and B's losses vs A) are averaged. priorGames depends on the "risk level": 3,000 / 2,000 / 1,000 / 500 / 250 for very-low … very-high. Ratings use the Elo transform r = −400·log10(1/w − 1) — [draftgap analysis.ts](https://github.com/vigovlugt/draftgap/blob/main/packages/core/src/draft/analysis.ts), [risk-level.ts](https://github.com/vigovlugt/draftgap/blob/main/packages/core/src/risk/risk-level.ts), [ratings.ts](https://github.com/vigovlugt/draftgap/blob/main/packages/core/src/rating/ratings.ts)
- Policy constraints on modelling opponents: Riot's general policies state "Products cannot de-anonymize players who cannot reasonably be identified from visible information" — [Riot developer policies](https://developer.riotgames.com/policies/general). A 2022 Riot post (from patch 12.22) requires apps to stop showing player names or stats in Ranked Solo/Duo champion select, naming Blitz and Porofessor as examples. The stated aim is to reduce negativity and pressure to dodge — [devtrackers (Riot, 2022)](https://devtrackers.gg/leagueoflegends/p/749a443a-apps-that-reveal-player-names-or-stats-in-champ-select-need-to-remove-these-informations-starting-patch-12-22)
- No pick order in public data: "Riot's API provides role assignments but not pick order" **[vendor]** — [LoLDraftAI suggestion-lift study](https://loldraftai.com/blog/suggestion-lift-study)

### Inferences
- **Enemy prior per role (guidance):** P(c | role r, band) = pick share of c in r ÷ (1 − ban rate of c), zeroed for champions banned or picked in this draft, then renormalised. This is the same availability correction as Lolalytics' PBI. Champions already locked by the enemy are assigned roles probabilistically (DraftGap-style enumeration), which identifies the likely lane opponent and the roles still open. LDC's ~50k-game bands are large enough for role pick shares, but not for most matchup cells; keep the shrinkage.
- **Counter-pick tilt:** for enemies who pick after the player, tilt the prior toward champions that beat the player's pick (the β term in section 2). With β small, the expectation stays close to the population view, matching the evidence that solo-queue picks are history-driven (~50% top-1 from personal history), not counter-driven.
- Don't build a per-enemy pick predictor: policy forbids identifying enemies, and without history the best LoL model gets only ~15% top-1. Ally hovers are the one strong signal (a direct intention). They are already used for "protect the hover" bans.

### Gaps
- No published LoL **solo-queue** next-pick model evaluated on Gold–Platinum, and no top-k number for "lane opponent given role" specifically, was found.
- No source quantifies how often solo-queue last pickers actually counter-pick (β). It would need LCU-recorded drafts.
- The exact definition of Summerville's 11.94% (top-1 vs top-3) is ambiguous in the extracted text.

## 5. Combining personal comfort with draft value, and recommendation diversity

### Takeaway
The one published personalised recommender with an outcome-oriented evaluation, DraftRec, does **not** add comfort and draft value. It **restricts** candidates to champions the player plausibly picks (selection probability > 0.02) and then takes the highest predicted win probability. That constrained rule beat both pure-comfort and pure-value ranking under a separate evaluator, and pure-value ranking was worst. Commercial tools (LoLTheory, LoLDraftAI, iTero) personalise through the player's own games, favourite stars, or an assumed experience level. Mastery effects are large next to draft effects (iTero: 41.5% under 5k mastery vs 54.5% at 500k+). No draft recommender found optimises diversity explicitly. Riot's policy requires multiple choices and more, not less, decision diversity.

### Cited Findings
- DraftRec strategies, LoL, scored by a separate evaluator (OptMatch). Comfort-only ranking by pick probability: HR@10 0.8836, NG@10 0.6618, predicted win 52.72% (top-3) / 52.46% (top-10). Value-only ranking: HR@10 0.2051, NG@10 0.0938, win 51.34% / 51.55%, the worst. Constrained (DraftRec+): HR@10 0.8495, NG@10 0.5657, win **54.12%** / 52.59% — [arXiv 2204.12750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12750)
- DraftRec user study (N = 84, players' own last 50 drafts replayed, 3 recommendations shown with attention-based "synergy/counter" explanations): 86.90% familiar with the recommended champions; 79.76% thought they would lead to victory (92.86% for the top-ranked group vs 73.68% for Silver); 80.95% found the explanations reasonable; 86.90% would use it — [arXiv 2204.12750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12750)
- iTero (1M+ solo-queue games, 2022): sub-5k-mastery players average 41.5% vs 54.5% at 500k+. 2.5k → 7.5k mastery gains "almost 8%"; 75k → 175k gains under one point. Advice: "Play at least 5 games on a champion before using it in Ranked", and among your most-played champions prefer the higher-win-rate ones **[vendor]** — [iTero solo queue draft analysis](https://www.itero.gg/articles/draft-sq)
- LoLTheory: Personalized mode uses "performance and game count for all champions from the last 10 patches"; Fixed mode assumes "25 games played on each champion"; Classic mode uses raw win rates. Its example (patch 13.1): Gragas jungle players averaged 14.6 games on Gragas vs 47.3 for Shaco players, and Gragas's win rate "would be 1.6% higher" with Shaco's experience distribution **[vendor]** — [LoLTheory personalized vs fixed](https://blog.loltheory.gg/personalized-and-fixed-recommendations/)
- LoLDraftAI: you star favourite champions per lane and "The model ranks every champion against your team and the enemy team" **[vendor]** — [LoLDraftAI](https://loldraftai.com)
- Heo, Kadiyam & Panthi (arXiv, May 2026), a player-conditional champion ranking that blends four sources: a population-strength proxy, style similarity (recency-weighted style vectors, weighted cosine), direct and indirect mastery priors, and archetype guardrails (k-means++). Scores are robust-normalised (median/MAD) and decomposed into performance, fit, mastery and archetype terms. **No quantitative evaluation** (a 100-game single-player sanity check only); it proposes temporal splits, next-champion recovery, calibration and ablations — [arXiv 2605.18338](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18338)
- Riot policy: "Products should increase, and not decrease the diversity of game decisions (builds, compositions, characters, decks)"; "should not remove game decisions, but may highlight decisions that are important" and "give multiple choices to help players make good decisions" — [Riot developer policies](https://developer.riotgames.com/policies/general)
- DraftArtist's proposed extension: augment the state with player information and train a win predictor on picks plus player-specific data — [arXiv 1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130)

### Inferences
- DraftRec's result supports LDC's "comfort dominates" design. The safer structure is a **gate plus rank**: candidates come from the player's owned and played pool (or a minimum-games threshold, cf. iTero's ≥5 games), then are ranked by draft value plus the personal term. An unbounded additive term lets a meta champion the player never plays outrank their pool, which is DraftRec's value-only failure mode. If LDC keeps the additive comfort term in rating points, champions with ~0 personal games should carry a negative experience offset large enough to keep them out of the top 3 except in "learning" contexts.
- The mastery curve (+8 points from 2.5k to 7.5k, flat after ~75k) is much steeper than typical matchup deltas (a few points). The personal term should be concave in games played: it rises fast over the first ~5–20 games and then saturates. This matches LDC's "comfort first without a forced main" preference.
- **Utility vs probability:** for a binary ranked outcome, expected utility equals win probability, so "regret" for a pick is simply (best predicted P(win) in the pool) − (P(win) of the pick). LoLDraftAI's "suggestion lift" is this quantity. It is a natural, explainable number for LDC's post-game card ("your pick cost ~1.2 pts vs the best option you play").
- **Diversity (guidance):** Riot's "multiple choices" rule is better served by three distinct options than by three near-duplicates. When the top candidates are within the model's uncertainty, label them as near-equal and diversify the shown set: one safe blind option, one counter option if available, one comfort pick. Don't present a strict #1 that never changes. Always surfacing the same champion would also reduce "diversity of game decisions".

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures how comfort and draft value should be weighted in solo queue from outcomes (e.g., a joint model of mastery × matchup). DraftRec learns it implicitly, and only for top-0.1% players.
- No draft recommender with an explicit diversity objective, or any measurement of recommendation diversity, was found.

## 6. Evaluating draft recommenders from observational data: why "followers of #1 won X%" is confounded, and better protocols

### Takeaway
Published evaluations fall into four tiers: (1) in-model simulation, where the same win model both chooses and scores (DraftArtist, JueWu, HoK); (2) separate-evaluator offline scoring (DraftRec with OptMatch); (3) out-of-time predictive evaluation of the win model itself (log loss, Brier, calibration on matches after the training cutoff; LoLDraftAI vs DraftGap); (4) human studies or real play (DraftRec N = 84; HoK vs pros, which mixes drafting with play). No source evaluates a LoL draft recommender's effect on **realised** solo-queue win rate with randomisation. Naive "players who followed our #1 won X%" comparisons are confounded. The offline-evaluation literature (Li et al. 2011) shows that unbiased replay needs logged randomisation.

### Cited Findings
- In-model evaluation: DraftArtist's metric is the mean predicted win rate from the same NN used as its reward, and JueWu's is "the averaged winning rate predicted by φ(s)" — the predictor the search maximises — [arXiv 1806.10130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.10130), [arXiv 2012.10171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10171)
- Separate evaluator: DraftRec used OptMatch to score recommendations because an identical model causes "correlation of inaccurate predictions". Under it, the value-greedy strategy that looked best to its own value head scored worst (51.34% vs 54.12% for the constrained strategy) — [arXiv 2204.12750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.12750)
- Out-of-time predictive evaluation: 32,750 ranked Emerald+ matches played after both data cutoffs, with log loss, accuracy, Brier and ECE, 95% bootstrap CIs, and slices by elo and region **[vendor-run]** — [LoLDraftAI vs DraftGap](https://loldraftai.com/blog/draftgap-vs-loldraftai-comparison). LoLDraftAI reports bucketed calibration on unseen drafts **[vendor]** — [LoLDraftAI explained](https://loldraftai.com/blog/loldraftai-explained)
- Counterfactual "lift" on held-out games: swap the actual pick for the top suggestion at the state the picker saw, using a realistic baseline (the "strongest-visible" slot) and a naive one. These are model-predicted outcomes; the post notes that earlier-pick lifts ignore opponent adaptation — [LoLDraftAI suggestion-lift study](https://loldraftai.com/blog/suggestion-lift-study)
- Tool comparison: DraftComPromise (Fraunhofer) was compared against four existing draft tools; "the established draft tools and DraftComPromise were close together in both win-rate statistics and the champions recommended" **[snippet; page blocked]** — [Fraunhofer publica](https://publica.fraunhofer.de/handle/publica/475693)
- Offline evaluation theory: Li, Chu, Langford & Wang (WSDM 2011). Offline evaluation is hard because of "partial-label" feedback: the outcome is seen only for the action actually taken. Simulators "introduce modeling bias"; their replay method is "provably unbiased" when logs come from randomised exploration, and its offline results tracked online buckets **[abstract/summary]** — [arXiv 1003.5956](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.5956)
- JueWu acknowledges the gap between its predictor's training population and real opponents as an unsolved limitation — [arXiv 2012.10171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.10171)

### Inferences
- **Why "games where the player picked our #1 won X%" is confounded:**
  - Selection: players follow the suggestion mostly when it is already their comfort pick, and comfort raises win rate on its own (iTero mastery curve).
  - Champion strength: the #1 is disproportionately a strong-in-band champion, whose base win rate is high whoever recommends it.
  - Draft state: "followable" suggestions occur in easier drafts (e.g., when the counter is available).
  - Leakage: if the engine was fit on the same games, it scores itself.
  - Reverse dependence: a player tilting or on a losing streak may ignore advice.
  Any of these can produce a positive gap with zero causal effect.
- **Statistical power:** draft effects of 1–3 points are tiny next to Bernoulli noise. Detecting a 2-point difference (52% vs 50%) at α = 0.05 and 80% power needs n ≈ (1.96 + 0.84)² × (0.25 + 0.25) / 0.02² ≈ **9,800 games per arm**. LDC's ≤10 users will never measure the realised effect of advice. Evaluation must rest on population data.
- **Recommended protocol for LDC (ordered by value):**
  1. Score the win model out of time per band: train on games before a cutoff, test after it, and report log loss, Brier and calibration (ECE, reliability buckets) against baselines (champion-strength-only, band-average). This mirrors the LoLDraftAI/DraftGap comparison and LDC's existing `backtest`.
  2. Check that each term (matchup, synergy, counters, blind expectation, ×1.5) improves held-out log loss. Drop or shrink any term that doesn't, the way DraftRec and DraftArtist found interaction terms add only ~0–2 points.
  3. Measure recommendation quality with a **separate** evaluator: a model of a different family trained on a disjoint split (e.g., LoLDraftAI-style masked neural net or plain logistic regression). Report predicted lift of top-1/top-3 over the actual pick and regret. Use LoLDraftAI's "strongest-visible" style baseline for pick-order realism, and report lifts at the player's actual pick position.
  4. Simulated drafts: replay held-out drafts with LDC's pick inserted and enemies drawn from the opponent model (priors plus tilt) instead of their real picks, as a sensitivity check on blind-pick logic. Label the results as model-predicted.
  5. If outcome evidence is ever wanted, randomise among near-tied top-3 orderings and log the propensities (Li et al.-style replay/IPS). This is only meaningful pooled across many users.
- Report everything as "model-predicted" in UI and docs. Never claim causal win-rate gains from following advice.

### Gaps
- No randomised or quasi-experimental study of draft-advice effects on realised win rates (in any MOBA) was found.
- No published evaluation of ban recommendations was found (see section 3).
- The DraftComPromise paper's actual numbers could not be read (bot protection).
- LoLDraftAI's comparison numbers come from a vendor-run evaluation with a self-ported competitor formula; no independent replication was found.
